# Verse 1

## Sanskrit
कर्तुराज्ञया प्राप्यते फलम् । कर्म किं परं कर्म तज्जडम् ॥ १ ॥

## IAST
karturājñayā prāpyate phalaṃ | karma kiṃ paraṃ karma tajjaḍam || 1 ||

## Translation
By the command of the Creator (or the Lord), the result of action is obtained. Can action be the Supreme? No, action is inert (insentient).

---

# Verse 2

## Sanskrit
कृतिमहोदधौ पतनकारणम् । फलमशाश्वतं गतिनिरोधकम् ॥ २ ॥

## IAST
kṛtimahodadhau patanakāraṇam | phalamaśāśvataṃ gatinirodhakam || 2 ||

## Translation
The fruit of action is transient and acts as the cause for falling into the great ocean of action. It is an obstruction to (spiritual) progress.

---

# Verse 3

## Sanskrit
ईश्वरार्पितं नेच्छया कृतम् । चित्तशोधकं मुक्तिसाधकम् ॥ ३ ॥

## IAST
īśvarārpitaṃ necchayā kṛtam | cittaśodhakaṃ muktisādhakam || 3 ||

## Translation
Action offered to the Lord and performed without desire is a purifier of the mind and serves as an aid to liberation.

---

# Verse 4

## Sanskrit
कायवाङ्मनः कार्यमुत्तमम् । पूजनं जपश्चिन्तनं क्रमात् ॥ ४ ॥

## IAST
kāyavāṅmanaḥ kāryamuttamam | pūjanaṃ japaścintanaṃ kramāt || 4 ||

## Translation
Worship, repetition of the Name (japa), and meditation—performed by body, speech, and mind respectively—are superior in that order.

---

# Verse 5

## Sanskrit
जगत ईशधी युक्तसेवनम् । अष्टमूर्तिभृद्देवपूजनम् ॥ ५ ॥

## IAST
jagata īśadhī yukta sevanaṃ | aśṭamūrtibhṛd devapūjanam || 5 ||

## Translation
Worship of the Lord in the form of the eight-fold manifestations (the world) is perfect worship.

---

# Verse 6

## Sanskrit
उत्तमस्तवादुच्चमन्दतः । चित्तजं जपध्यानमुत्तमम् ॥ ६ ॥

## IAST
uttama-stavād-ucca-mandataḥ | cittajaṃ japa dhyānam uttamam || 6 ||

## Translation
Better than hymns of praise is repetition of the Name; better low-voiced than loud, but best of all is meditation in the mind.

---

# Verse 7

## Sanskrit
आज्यधारया स्रोतसा समम् । सरलचिन्तनं विरलतः परम् ॥ ७ ॥

## IAST
ājyadhārayā srotasā samam | sarala cintanaṃ viralataḥ param || 7 ||

## Translation
Better than broken meditation is continuous current, steady as a stream or a flow of oil.

---

# Verse 8

## Sanskrit
भेदभावनात् सोऽहमित्यसौ । भावनाऽभिदा पावनी मता ॥ ८ ॥

## IAST
bhedabhāvanāt so'hamityasau | bhāvanābhidā pāvanī matā || 8 ||

## Translation
Better than viewing Him as "Other" is to hold Him as the "I" within; this non-dual contemplation is the most purifying.

---

# Verse 9

## Sanskrit
भावशून्यसद्भावसुस्थितिः । भावनाबलाद्भक्तिरुत्तमा ॥ ९ ॥

## IAST
bhāvaśūnyasad bhāvasusthitiḥ | bhāvanābalād bhaktiruttamā || 9 ||

## Translation
Abidance in pure Being, transcending thought through intense love, is the essence of supreme devotion.

---

# Verse 10

## Sanskrit
हृत्स्थले मनः स्वस्थता क्रिया । भक्तियोगबोधाश्च निश्चितम् ॥ १० ॥

## IAST
hṛtsthale manaḥ svasthatā kriyā | bhakti yoga bodhaśca niścitam || 10 ||

## Translation
Absorption in the Heart, the Source of Being, is the true path of Action, Devotion, and Knowledge.

---

# Verse 11

## Sanskrit
वायुरोधनाल्लीयते मनः । जालपक्षिवद्रोधसाधनम् ॥ ११ ॥

## IAST
vāyurodhanāllīyate manaḥ | jālapakṣivadrodhasādhanam || 11 ||

## Translation
Breath-restraint causes the mind to subside, like a bird caught in a net. This is a means to absorption.

---

# Verse 12

## Sanskrit
चित्तवायवश्चित्क्रियायुताः । शाखयोर्द्वयी शक्तिमूलका ॥ १२ ॥

## IAST
cittavāyavaścitkriyāyutāḥ | śākhayordvayī śaktimūlakā || 12 ||

## Translation
Mind and breath (thought and action) fork out like two branches, but both have a single root (source).

---

# Verse 13

## Sanskrit
लयविनाशने उभयरोधने । लयगतं पुनर्भवति नो मृतम् ॥ १३ ॥

## IAST
layavināśane ubhayarodhane | layagataṃ punarbhavati no mṛtam || 13 ||

## Translation
Absorption (laya) and destruction (vinasha) are the two types of restraint. That which is absorbed will rise again; that which is destroyed does not return.

---

# Verse 14

## Sanskrit
प्राणबन्धनाल्लीनमानसम् । एकचिन्तनान्नाशमेत्यदः ॥ १४ ॥

## IAST
prāṇabandhanāllīnamānasam | ekacintanānnāśametyadaḥ || 14 ||

## Translation
When the mind is absorbed through breath-control, it is destroyed by one-pointed focus.

---

# Verse 15

## Sanskrit
नष्टमानसोत्कृष्टयोगिनः । कृत्यमस्ति किं स्वस्थितिं यतः ॥ १५ ॥

## IAST
naṣṭamānasotkṛṣṭyoginaḥ | kṛtyamasti kiṃ svasthitiṃ yataḥ || 15 ||

## Translation
For the noble yogi whose mind has been destroyed, what action remains? He has reached his own natural state.

---

# Verse 16

## Sanskrit
दृश्यवारितं चित्तमात्मनः । चित्त्वदर्शनं तत्त्वदर्शनम् ॥ १६ ॥

## IAST
dṛśyavāritaṃ cittamātmanaḥ | cittvadarśanaṃ tattvadarśanam || 16 ||

## Translation
When the mind is withdrawn from objects and perceives its own nature as Consciousness, that is true vision (Self-Realization).

---

# Verse 17

## Sanskrit
मानसं तु किं मार्गणे कृते । नैव मानसं मार्ग आर्जवात् ॥ १७ ॥

## IAST
mānasaṃ tu kiṃ mārgaṇe kṛte | naiva mānasaṃ mārga ārjavāt || 17 ||

## Translation
When one inquires "What is the mind?", it is found that there is no such thing as "mind" (as an independent entity). This is the direct path.

---

# Verse 18

## Sanskrit
वृत्तयस्त्वहं वृत्तिमाश्रिताः । वृत्तयो मनो विद्धयहं मनः ॥ १८ ॥

## IAST
vṛttayastvahaṃ vṛttimāśritāḥ | vṛttayo mano viddhyahaṃ manaḥ || 18 ||

## Translation
Thoughts are the mind; they all revolve around the "I-thought." The mind is only the "I-thought."

---

# Verse 19

## Sanskrit
अहमयं कुतो भवति चिन्वतः । अयि पतत्यहं निजविचारणम् ॥ १९ ॥

## IAST
ahamayaṃ kuto bhavati cinvataḥ | ayi patatyahaṃ nijavicāraṇam || 19 ||

## Translation
When one searches within, "Whence does this 'I' arise?", the "I" vanishes. This is the search for the Self (Self-Enquiry).

---

# Verse 20

## Sanskrit
अहमि नाशभाजयहमहंतया । स्फुरति हृत्स्वयं परमपूर्णसत् ॥ २० ॥

## IAST
ahamai nāśabhājyahamahaṃtayā | sphurati hṛtsvayaṃ paramapūrṇasat || 20 ||

## Translation
When the "I" (ego) dies, the Supreme Self (the Fullness of Being) shines forth spontaneously in the Heart as "I-I."

---

# Verse 21

## Sanskrit
इदमहं पदाऽभिख्यमन्वहम् । अहमिलीनकेऽप्यलय सत्तया ॥ २१ ॥

## IAST
idam ahaṁpadā’bhikhyamanvaham | ahamilīnakē’pyalayasattayā || 21 ||

## Translation
The word "I" always refers to the Heart (the Self), which is the undissolvable existence, even when the ego "I" is dissolved.

---

# Verse 22

## Sanskrit
विग्रहेन्द्रिय प्राणधीतमः । नाहमेकसत् तज्जडं ह्यसत् ॥ २२ ॥

## IAST
vigrahēndriyaprāṇa-dhītamaḥ | nāhamēkasattajjaḍaṃ hyasat || 22 ||

## Translation
The body, senses, mind, life-force, and ignorance (the five sheaths) are not "I," the one existence, because they are insentient and unreal.

---

# Verse 23

## Sanskrit
सत्त्वभासिका चित्क्ववेतरा । सत्तया हि चिच्चित्तया ह्यहम् ॥ २३ ॥

## IAST
sattvabhāsikā chitkvavētarā | sattayā hi chichchittayā hyaham || 23 ||

## Translation
Since there is no other knower to know That (the Self), existence is consciousness, and that consciousness is "I."

---

# Verse 24

## Sanskrit
ईशजीवयोर्वेषधीभिदा । सत्स्वभावतो वस्तु केवलम् ॥ २४ ॥

## IAST
īśajīvayōrvēṣadhībhidā | satsvabhāvatō vastu kēvalam || 24 ||

## Translation
In their true nature, the creator (Ishvara) and the creature (Jiva) are the same; only their adjuncts (the limiting forms/identities) differ.

---

# Verse 25

## Sanskrit
वेषहानतः स्वात्मदर्शनम् । ईशदर्शनं स्वात्मरूपतः ॥ २५ ॥

## IAST
vēṣahānataḥ svātmadarśanam | īśadarśanaṃ svātmarūpataḥ || 25 ||

## Translation
Realizing the Self, after discarding the adjuncts, is seeing the Lord, as He shines as the pure Self.

---

# Verse 26

## Sanskrit
आत्मसंस्थितिः स्वात्मदर्शनम् । आत्मनिद्वयादात्मनिष्ठता ॥ २६ ॥

## IAST
ātmasaṃsthitiḥ svātmadarśanam | ātmanirdvayādātmaniṣṭhatā || 26 ||

## Translation
To know the Self is to be the Self, for it is non-dual; abiding in this state is true Self-realization.

---

# Verse 27

## Sanskrit
ज्ञानवर्जिताऽज्ञानहीनचित् । ज्ञानमस्ति किं ज्ञातुमन्तरम् ॥ २७ ॥

## IAST
jñānavarjitā’jñānahīnachit | jñānamasti kiṃ jñātumantaram || 27 ||

## Translation
That is true Knowledge which is beyond both knowledge (of objects) and ignorance; there is no object to be known there.

---

# Verse 28

## Sanskrit
किं स्वरूपमित्यात्मदर्शने । अव्ययाऽभवा’’पूर्णचित्सुखम् ॥ २८ ॥

## IAST
kiṃ svarūpamityātmadarśanē | avyayā’bhavā’’pūrṇachitsukham || 28 ||

## Translation
Upon enquiring "What is my nature?" and realizing the Self, one experiences the undying, eternal, and complete consciousness-bliss.

---

# Verse 29

## Sanskrit
बन्धमुक्त्यतीतं परं सुखम् । विन्दतीह जीवस्तु दैविकः ॥ २९ ॥

## IAST
bandhamuktyatītaṃ paraṃ sukham | vindatīha jīvastu daivikaḥ || 29 ||

## Translation
One who has attained this state is truly divine, finding the supreme bliss that is beyond both bondage and liberation.

---

# Verse 30

## Sanskrit
अहमपेतकं निजविभानकम् । महदिदंतपो रमणवागियम् ॥ ३० ॥

## IAST
ahamapētakaṃ nijavibhānakam | mahadidantapō ramanavāgiyam || 30 ||

## Translation
The destruction of the ego "I," which reveals the Self, is the greatest austerity. This is the teaching of Ramana.

---
