chapter: "Chapter 16: Svasthya"
subtitle: "Self-Abidance through Obliteration of the World"
synopsis: "Ashtavakra instructs further that the beatitude of the Atman is gained by merely forgetting everything (all the non-self). The state of supreme bliss is the state of objectless awareness devoid of effort of any kind. To abide in it, one must remain freed of likes and dislikes, 'I and mine' and thoughts. "

# Verse 16.1

## Sanskrit
अष्टावक्र उवाच 
आचक्ष्व शृणु वा तात
नानाशास्त्राण्यनेकशः।
तथापि न तव स्वास्थ्यं
सर्वविस्मरणाद् ऋते॥

## IAST
asht'aavakra uvaacha 
aachakshva shrinu vaa taata
naanaashaastraanyanekashah.
tathaapi na tava svaasthyam
sarvavismaranaad rite..

## Translation
Ashtavakra said: My son, you may recite or listen to countless scriptures, but you will not be established within until you can forget everything.

# Verse 16.2

## Sanskrit
भोगं कर्म समाधिं वा
कुरु विज्ञ तथापि ते।
चित्तं निरस्तसर्वाशाम-
त्यर्थं रोचयिष्यति॥

## IAST
bhogam karma samaadhim vaa
kuru vijnya tathaapi te.
chittam nirastasarvaashaama-
tyartham rochayishyati..

## Translation
You may, as a learned man, indulge in wealth, activity, and meditation, but your mind will still long for that which is the cessation of desire, and beyond all goals.

---

# Verse 16.3

## Sanskrit
आयासात्सकलो दुःखी
नैनं जानाति कश्चन।
अनेनैवोपदेशेन धन्यः
प्राप्नोति निर्वृतिम्॥

## IAST
aayaasaatsakalo duhkhee
nainam jaanaati kashchana.
anenaivopadeshena dhanyah
praapnoti nirvritim..

## Translation
Everyone is in pain because of their striving to achieve something, but no one realises it. By no more than this instruction, the fortunate one attains tranquillity.

---

# Verse 16.4

## Sanskrit
व्यापारे खिद्यते यस्तु
निमेषोन्मेषयोरपि।
तस्यालस्य धुरीणस्य
सुखं नन्यस्य कस्यचित्॥

## IAST
vyaapaare khidyate yastu
nimeshonmeshayorapi.
tasyaalasya dhureenasya
sukham nanyasya kasyachit..

## Translation
Happiness belongs to no one but that supremely lazy man for whom even opening and closing his eyes is a bother.

---

# Verse 16.5

## Sanskrit
इदं कृतमिदं नेति
द्वंद्वैर्मुक्तं यदा मनः।
धर्मार्थकाममोक्षेषु
निरपेक्षं तदा भवेत्॥

## IAST
idam kritamidam neti
dvandvairmuktam yadaa manah.
dharmaarthakaamamoksheshu
nirapeksham tadaa bhavet..

## Translation
When the mind is freed from such pairs of opposites as, “I have done this,” and “I have not done that,” it becomes indifferent to merit, wealth, sensuality and liberation.

---

# Verse 16.6

## Sanskrit
विरक्तो विषयद्वेष्टा
रागी विषयलोलुपः।
ग्रहमोक्षविहीनस्तु
न विरक्तो न रागवान्॥

## IAST
virakto vishayadvesht'aa
raagee vishayalolupah.
grahamokshaviheenastu
na virakto na raagavaan..

## Translation
One man is abstemious and averse to the senses, another is greedy and attached to them, but he who is free from both taking and rejecting is neither abstemious nor greedy.

---

# Verse 16.7

## Sanskrit
हेयोपादेयता तावत्-
संसारविटपांकुरः।
स्पृहा जीवति यावद् वै
निर्विचारदशास्पदम्॥

## IAST
heyopaadeyataa taavat-
samsaaravit'apaankurah.
sprihaa jeevati yaavad vai
nirvichaaradashaaspadam..

## Translation
So long as desire, the state of lack of discrimination, remains, the sense of revulsion and attraction will remain, which is the root and branch of samsara.

---

# Verse 16.8

## Sanskrit
प्रवृत्तौ जायते रागो
निर्वृत्तौ द्वेष एव हि।
निर्द्वन्द्वो बालवद् धीमान्
एवमेव व्यवस्थितः॥

## IAST
pravrittau jaayate raago
nirvrittau dvesha eva hi.
nirdvandvo baalavad dheemaan
evameva vyavasthitah..

## Translation
Desire springs from usage, and aversion from abstension, but the wise man is free from the pairs of opposites like a child, and becomes established.

---

# Verse 16.9

## Sanskrit
हातुमिच्छति संसारं
रागी दुःखजिहासया।
वीतरागो हि निर्दुःखस्-
तस्मिन्नपि न खिद्यति॥

## IAST
haatumichchhati samsaaram
raagee duhkhajihaasayaa.
veetaraago hi nirduhkhas-
tasminnapi na khidyati..

## Translation
The passionate man wants to eliminate samsara so as to avoid pain, but the dispassionate man is free from pain and feels no distress even in it.

---

# Verse 16.10

## Sanskrit
यस्याभिमानो मोक्षेऽपि
देहेऽपि ममता तथा।
न च ज्ञानी न वा योगी
केवलं दुःखभागसौ॥

## IAST
yasyaabhimaano mokshe'pi
dehe'pi mamataa tathaa.
na cha jnyaanee na vaa yogee
kevalam duhkhabhaagasau..

## Translation
He who is proud about even liberation or his own body, and feels them his own, is neither a seer nor a yogi. He is still just a sufferer.

---

# Verse 16.11

## Sanskrit
हरो यद्युपदेष्टा ते हरिः
कमलजोऽपि वा।
तथापि न तव स्वाथ्यं
सर्वविस्मरणादृते॥

## IAST
haro yadyupadesht'aa te harih
kamalajo'pi vaa.
tathaapi na tava svaathyam
sarvavismaranaadrite..

## Translation
If even Shiva, Vishnu, or the lotus-born Brahma were your instructor, until you have forgotten everything you cannot be established within.
