# Verse 3.1

## Sanskrit
अष्टावक्र उवाच 
अविनाशिनमात्मानं
एकं विज्ञाय तत्त्वतः।
तवात्मज्ञानस्य धीरस्य
कथमर्थार्जने रतिः॥

## IAST
asht'aavakra uvaacha 
avinaashinamaatmaanam
ekam vijnyaaya tattvatah.
tavaatmajnyaanasya dheerasya
kathamarthaarjane ratih..

## Translation
Ashtavakra said: Knowing yourself as truly one and indestructible, how could a wise man possessing self-knowledge like you feel any pleasure in acquiring wealth?

---

# Verse 3.2

## Sanskrit
आत्माज्ञानादहो
प्रीतिर्विषयभ्रमगोचरे।
शुक्तेरज्ञानतो लोभो
यथा रजतविभ्रमे॥

## IAST
aatmaajnyaanaadaho
preetirvishayabhramagochare.
shukterajnyaanato lobho
yathaa rajatavibhrame..

## Translation
Truly, when one does not know oneself, one takes pleasure in the objects of mistaken perception, just as greed arises for the mistaken silver in one who does not know mother of pearl for what it is.

---

# Verse 3.3

## Sanskrit
विश्वं स्फुरति यत्रेदं
तरङ्गा इव सागरे।
सोऽहमस्मीति विज्ञाय
किं दीन इव धावसि॥

## IAST
vishvam sphurati yatredam
tarangaa iva saagare.
so'hamasmeeti vijnyaaya
kim deena iva dhaavasi..

## Translation
All this wells up like waves in the sea. Recognising, “I am That,” why run around like someone in need?

---

# Verse 3.4

## Sanskrit
श्रुत्वापि शुद्धचैतन्य
आत्मानमतिसुन्दरं।
उपस्थेऽत्यन्तसंसक्तो
मालिन्यमधिगच्छति॥

## IAST
shrutvaapi shuddhachaitanya
aatmaanamatisundaram.
upasthe'tyantasamsakto
maalinyamadhigachchhati..

## Translation
After hearing of oneself as pure consciousness and the supremely beautiful, is one to go on lusting after sordid sexual objects?

---

# Verse 3.5

## Sanskrit
सर्वभूतेषु चात्मानं
सर्वभूतानि चात्मनि।
मुनेर्जानत आश्चर्यं
ममत्वमनुवर्तते॥

## IAST
sarvabhooteshu chaatmaanam
sarvabhootaani chaatmani.
munerjaanata aashcharyam
mamatvamanuvartate..

## Translation
When the sage has realised that he himself is in all beings, and all beings are in him, it is astonishing that the sense of individuality should be able to continue.

---

# Verse 3.6

## Sanskrit
आस्थितः परमाद्वैतं
मोक्षार्थेऽपि व्यवस्थितः।
आश्चर्यं कामवशगो
विकलः केलिशिक्षया॥

## IAST
aasthitah paramaadvaitam
mokshaarthe'pi vyavasthitah.
aashcharyam kaamavashago
vikalah kelishikshayaa..

## Translation
It is astonishing that a man who has reached the supreme nondual state and is intent on the benefits of liberation should still be subject to lust and in bondage to sexual activity.

---

# Verse 3.7

## Sanskrit
उद्भूतं ज्ञानदुर्मित्रम-
वधार्यातिदुर्बलः।
आश्चर्यं काममाकाङ्क्षेत् कालमन्तमनुश्रितः॥

## IAST
udbhootam jnyaanadurmitrama-
vadhaaryaatidurbalah.
aashcharyam kaamamaakaankshet kaalamantamanushritah..

## Translation
It is astonishing that one already very debilitated, and knowing very well that its arousal is the enemy of knowledge, should still hanker after sensuality, even when approaching his last days.

---

# Verse 3.8

## Sanskrit
इहामुत्र विरक्तस्य नित्यानित्यविवेकिनः।
आश्चर्यं मोक्षकामस्य
मोक्षाद् एव विभीषिका॥

## IAST
ihaamutra viraktasya nityaanityavivekinah.
aashcharyam mokshakaamasya
mokshaad eva vibheeshikaa..

## Translation
It is astonishing that one who is unattached to the things of this world or the next, who discriminates between the permanent and the impermanent, and who longs for liberation, should still be afraid of liberation.

---

# Verse 3.9

## Sanskrit
धीरस्तु भोज्यमानोऽपि पीड्यमानोऽपि सर्वदा।
आत्मानं केवलं पश्यन्
न तुष्यति न कुप्यति॥

## IAST
dheerastu bhojyamaano'pi peed'yamaano'pi sarvadaa.
aatmaanam kevalam pashyan
na tushyati na kupyati..

## Translation
Whether feted or tormented, the wise man is always aware of his supreme self-nature and is neither pleased nor disappointed.

---

# Verse 3.10

## Sanskrit
चेष्टमानं शरीरं स्वं पश्यत्यन्यशरीरवत्।
संस्तवे चापि निन्दायां
कथं क्षुभ्येत् महाशयः॥

## IAST
chesht'amaanam shareeram svam pashyatyanyashareeravat.
samstave chaapi nindaayaam
katham kshubhyet mahaashayah..

## Translation
The great-souled person sees even his own body in action as if it were someone else’s, so how should he be disturbed by praise or blame?

---

# Verse 3.11

## Sanskrit
मायामात्रमिदं विश्वं
पश्यन् विगतकौतुकः।
अपि सन्निहिते मृत्यौ
कथं त्रस्यति धीरधीः॥

## IAST
maayaamaatramidam vishvam
pashyan vigatakautukah.
api sannihite mrityau
katham trasyati dheeradheeh..

## Translation
Seeing this world as pure illusion, and devoid of any interest in it, how should the strong-minded person feel fear, even at the approach of death?

---

# Verse 3.12

## Sanskrit
निःस्पृहं मानसं यस्य
नैराश्येऽपि महात्मनः।
तस्यात्मज्ञानतृप्तस्य
तुलना केन जायते॥

## IAST
nihspriham maanasam yasya
nairaashye'pi mahaatmanah.
tasyaatmajnyaanatriptasya
tulanaa kena jaayate..

## Translation
Who can be compared to the great-souled person whose mind is free from desire even in disappointment, and who has found satisfaction in self-knowledge?

---

# Verse 3.13

## Sanskrit
स्वभावाद् एव जानानो
दृश्यमेतन्न किंचन।
इदं ग्राह्यमिदं त्याज्यं
स किं पश्यति धीरधीः॥

## IAST
svabhaavaad eva jaanaano
drishyametanna kinchana.
idam graahyamidam tyaajyam
sa kim pashyati dheeradheeh..

## Translation
How should a strong-minded person who knows that what he sees is by its very nature nothing, consider one thing to be grasped and another to be rejected?

---

# Verse 3.14

## Sanskrit
अंतस्त्यक्तकषायस्य
निर्द्वन्द्वस्य निराशिषः।
यदृच्छयागतो भोगो
न दुःखाय न तुष्टये॥

## IAST
antastyaktakashaayasya
nirdvandvasya niraashishah.
yadrichchhayaagato bhogo
na duhkhaaya na tusht'aye..

## Translation
An object of enjoyment that comes of itself is neither painful nor pleasurable for someone who has eliminated attachment, and who is free from dualism and from desire.
